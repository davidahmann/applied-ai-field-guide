## Portable synthesis

1. **A pilot needs a declared graduation event.** Set its timebox, evidence cutoff, separate technical/operator/adoption/value/economics/production gates, decision owner, and stop path before delivery begins. This sharpens the existing `FDE-003` contract; it does not justify free pilots or a universal duration.
2. **Activation is an operating result, not a launch event.** Stable eligible use, accepted outcomes, operator control, and support ownership matter after deployment. The guide already carries these through `ADP-001`, `ADP-002`, `OPS-004`, and `OPS-006`.
3. **Continuation has organizational and commercial precursors.** Sponsor continuity, receiving-team capability, funding or renewal timing, support load, and cost trajectory help route attention. They are not substitutes for independently accepted outcomes or attributable realized value.
4. **Portfolio economics should expose delivery reality.** Time to first accepted outcome, time to first accepted value, pilot-to-production flow, full delivery cost, target-specific effort, reusable capability, operating load, and stopped work help an FDE or internal applied-AI organization decide where to invest. Cohort definitions and accounting boundaries must be explicit; no external benchmark is imported.
5. **Field learning compounds only when custom effort falls responsibly.** A reusable artifact must preserve outcome, safety, adoption, supportability, and local policy ownership across independent contexts. Copying customer data or hiding required local work is not reuse.
6. **Professional integrity survives commercial pressure.** Minimize and protect customer context, report negative and inconclusive evidence, avoid manufacturing dependence, consider affected workers and operators, and decline requests that exceed lawful or authorized boundaries.

## External and internal equivalents

| External FDE/provider language | Internal applied-AI equivalent | Common evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paid deployment or renewal | Continued sponsorship, funding, or roadmap commitment | Continuation signal; not realized value proof |
| Expansion revenue | New workflow, segment, or authority investment | Requires a new target-specific gate and release decision |
| Delivery contribution | Value-to-full-cost and capacity allocation | Accounting boundary must be declared |
| Customer champion | Business sponsor or operating owner | Must have accountable backup and receiving ownership |
| Customer-specific customization | Business-unit-specific policy, integration, or support work | Remains local unless sanitized recurrence and validation support reuse |

## What this note does not support

- Universal conversion, time-to-value, margin, renewal, or reuse targets
- Free pilots as a default acquisition strategy
- Outcome-based pricing without attribution, dispute, cash-flow, and risk analysis
- Treating contract value, renewal, sponsorship, usage, or references as business-value evidence
- Selecting “lighthouse” customers at the expense of workflow fit, operator welfare, safety, or confidentiality
- Copying or commercially adapting the reviewed book without the author's permission
